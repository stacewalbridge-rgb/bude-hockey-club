Code of Ethics & Behaviour

Bude Hockey Club's standards of fair play, respect and responsibility.

← Back to Policies & Safety

This is a web-readable presentation of the Code of Ethics & Behaviour supplied by Bude Hockey Club. The club applies England Hockey's Code of Ethics & Behaviour and the Rules of Hockey.

Who the Code applies to

All members, coaches, managers, volunteers, players under and over 18, parents, spectators, umpires, officials and medical or allied health professionals. Members confirm their commitment when registering and renewing. Under-18 players and their parents or carers must both confirm.

Core principle

Everyone is personally responsible for their actions and must treat teammates, opposition players, coaches, volunteers, spectators and officials with respect before, during and after hockey.

Before a match

  • Attend training regularly and communicate availability to the captain or vice-captain seven days ahead.
  • Arrive punctually and notify the captain promptly of delays.
  • Bring protective equipment, necessary medication and the correct kit: sky-blue shirt, navy or black shorts or skirt and maroon socks.
  • Set a positive example, including appropriate language around young people.

During a match

  • Respect the captain's responsibility for team conduct and treat opposition players fairly.
  • Do not argue with umpires. Raise queries through the captain at half-time or after the match.
  • Do not criticise, swear at or belittle teammates; support each other.
  • Set a positive example for younger players and spectators.

After a match

  • Accept victory and defeat with dignity; thank umpires and opponents.
  • Join teammates and opposition for post-match tea, as set out in the club's Code.
  • Pay match fees to the captain or nominated person on match day.
  • Provide availability for the next match and continue to set a positive example.

Discipline

Breaches are considered by the Club Disciplinary Committee under the Disciplinary & Behaviour Policy. Team managers, captains and parents help explain the Code to junior members.
