Bude Hockey Club Safeguarding Policy

Creating and maintaining a safe and positive environment for everyone involved in hockey.

Need safeguarding help?The Club Welfare Officer is the first point of contact for young people, parents/carers, volunteers and staff. Bude Hockey Club’s current Welfare Officer is Tammy Snowsill.

Introduction

This policy document sets out the policy adopted by Bude Hockey Club (the Club) in order to ensure that it creates and maintains a safe and positive environment for all people involved in hockey.

The Club adopts England Hockey’s (EH) policy on Safeguarding and Protecting young people in hockey, together with the associated policies and procedures.

England Hockey safeguarding regulations ↗

Definitions

Hockey family – all individuals, clubs, associations and other organisations involved in any capacity in the game of hockey, whether or not they are members of EH. For clarity, this includes all players and anyone working within hockey – including all coaches, umpires, referees and other officials. This includes anyone who is paid to provide hockey related services – employee, self-employed or other work basis and volunteers.

Young people – anyone under the age of 18 years.

Implementation of the policy

The Club will implement the Safeguarding Policy through the following actions:

  • We recognise that all young people participating in hockey have the right to play in an environment free from poor practice, abuse and harm (regardless of age, gender, race, religion, sexual orientation, ability or disability).
  • We will ensure that all people working in any capacity at, or for, our Club have a responsibility for safeguarding young people and understand how the safeguarding policy and guidance applies to them; this applies to staff, officials, team managers, coaches and volunteers.
  • We will ensure that all individuals working within hockey at, or for, the Club are recruited in accordance with EH’s recruitment guidance. We will ensure that such individuals are aware of, and adhere to, the Club’s Code of Ethics and Behaviour.
  • We will ensure that we approach safeguarding with a young person’s perspective, always ensuring that the individual’s wellbeing is the prime focus.
  • We will ensure that the Club has a Club Welfare Officer – this person must have completed a Time To Listen course provided by EH.

Reporting

The Club will use EH’s Safeguarding and Protecting reporting procedures.

Welfare Officer

The Club’s Welfare Officer is a role that is part of the Executive Committee of the Club. The Welfare Officer is responsible for ensuring that all relevant volunteers in the Club have an up-to-date DBS check with a child barred list check included. This check must be performed every three years.

England Hockey DBS checks are performed through a company called First Advantage. DBS checks completed through hockey clubs and areas are portable within hockey. England Hockey do not accept DBS checks performed outside of member clubs and areas unless the individual has signed up to the DBS Government Update Service and is enhanced with a child barred list check included. All status checks must be performed through England Hockey centrally.

The Club Welfare Officer is the first point of contact for young people, parents/carers and volunteers or staff within the Club.

The Welfare Officer is the main point of contact for the England Hockey Safeguarding team, as well as the local Cornwall Hockey safeguarding volunteers.

The Welfare Officer is the Club’s source of procedural advice for safeguarding matters – this includes the members and the committees.
